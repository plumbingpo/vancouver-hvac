Why Hire a Professional for HVAC Maintenance?

Homeowners can take care of a short list: changing filters on time, keeping the outdoor coil clear, leaving registers open, and checking the drain. The rest takes instruments, because the problems that leave you stranded look perfectly normal. A capacitor that has lost 20 percent of its rating looks the same as a new one. Low charge shows up only in superheat and subcooling readings. Cracked heat exchangers, weak flame signals, marginal pressure switches, and amp draws creeping toward the breaker's limit are all invisible until measured, and all of them cost less to fix before they fail.

The gas side raises the stakes past money. Burners, flame proving, venting, and draft need annual instrumented verification because the failure mode is not just a cold house, it is combustion products where they do not belong. Maintenance Tips for HVAC Maintenance

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Change the filter every one to three months; it is the single highest-value habit there is
  • Pay attention to new noises or longer cycles and mention them at your next visit
  • Get your tune-ups on the calendar during mild weather, well before the rush
  • Trim plants back from the outdoor unit and give the coil a gentle rinse every spring
  • Test carbon monoxide detectors monthly if any of your equipment burns fuel
  • Leave every supply and return register open, including those in unused rooms
  • Clear the condensate drain early in cooling season, before algae has a chance to grow

No two heating systems break the same way, and in Vancouver homes the first question on any no-heat call is what kind of equipment we are dealing with. Furnaces most often quit on an oxidized flame sensor. The burners fire, the sensor fails to confirm the flame, and after three attempts the control board closes the gas valve and locks the system out. Cracked hot surface igniters, sticking pressure switches, plugged condensate traps on high-efficiency units, and tired blower motors round out the usual list. Heat pumps have their own failure patterns. A defrost cycle that underperforms lets ice build up on the outdoor coil, a bad reversing valve leaves the system stuck in cooling mode, and a dead outdoor unit hands the whole load to the electric backup strips, which cost several times as much to run.

Why Hire a Professional for Heating Repair?

The most serious heating failures are the ones nobody notices. A cracked heat exchanger can send combustion gases into the supply air, a blocked or failing flue can spill carbon monoxide into the house, and a furnace that lights late booms gas inside the exchanger, weakening it a little more every cycle. An untrained eye rarely catches any of it. Even routine symptoms can mislead: a short-cycling furnace may have a bad limit switch, or it may simply be shutting down to protect itself from a choked filter. And a flame sensor cleaned without a look at why it fouled will foul again.

Instruments and judgment are what our experienced technicians bring to both sides of that problem. We measure the flame signal instead of assuming it, inspect the heat exchanger, verify draft at the flue, and check temperature rise against the data plate so blower speed and burner output are matched. With heat pumps, the defrost board, sensors, and reversing valve get tested before anyone condemns a compressor, and we look at whether the backup strips have been running up your bill for weeks. Each repair we complete is proven safe and correct over a full heating cycle, which is the standard you should expect from anyone working on the equipment that keeps your family warm.

How to Prevent Heating Repair Problems

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Keep chlorine products, paints, and solvents away from the furnace, because their fumes corrode burners
  • Have the system serviced each autumn, before the weather tests every weak component
  • Test carbon monoxide detectors monthly and put one on every level of the home
  • Give the outdoor heat pump unit open space, free of snow drifts and plantings, so it can defrost
  • Do not ignore a boom at ignition or a new rattle, since early repairs cost far less
  • Replace filters monthly through the heating season to keep airflow strong and the heat exchanger from overheating
  • Keep the area around the furnace clear so the burner gets adequate combustion air
